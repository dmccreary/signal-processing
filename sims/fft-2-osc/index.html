    Edit in the p5.js Editor

    Description

    This MicroSim adds two oscillators, a sine wave and a cosine wave, plays the sum through your speakers, and shows it in two ways at once:

    \[x(t) = A_1 \sin(2\pi f_1 t) + A_2 \cos(2\pi f_2 t)\]

    The top plot (green) is the time domain: a 20 ms window of the sum, triggered at a rising zero crossing so a periodic sum holds still like an oscilloscope trace. The read-out gives the largest value of \(|x(t)|\) in the window. The vertical range is \(\pm 1\), and switches to \(\pm 2\) when \(A_1 + A_2\) is larger than 1. The bottom plot (purple) is the frequency domain: the magnitude spectrum from 0 to 2100 Hz computed by a Fast Fourier Transform (FFT). Gold markers and the read-out give the frequency and height of the two strongest peaks.

    • Sine Freq and Sine Amp (20-2000 Hz, default 440 Hz; 0-1, default 0.50) set \(f_1\) and \(A_1\).
    • Cosine Freq and Cosine Amp (20-2000 Hz, default 880 Hz; 0-1, default 0.30) set \(f_2\) and \(A_2\).
    • Start/Pause turns both tones on and off. Mute silences the speakers while the plots keep running.

    Each oscillator produces its own peak, at its own frequency, with a height proportional to its own amplitude. This is superposition: the spectrum of a sum is the sum of the spectra. The two waves start at the same instant, the cosine a quarter cycle ahead of the sine. Moving a frequency slider changes their relative phase, which changes the shape of the sum but not the magnitude spectrum.

    This MicroSim supports Chapter 7: DFT, FFT and Frequency Domain Analysis.

    The Fast Fourier Transform

    The FFT is an algorithm that computes the Discrete Fourier Transform (DFT), which converts a block of \(N\) samples from the time domain into \(N\) frequency bins. It reduces the work from \(O(N^2)\) to \(O(N \log N)\) operations, which is what makes real-time spectrum displays like this one possible. This MicroSim uses \(N = 16384\), so the bins are \(\Delta f = f_s / N\) apart: 2.93 Hz when your audio device runs at \(f_s = 48\) kHz (2.69 Hz at 44.1 kHz). The sample rate and bin width are printed under the spectrum on wide screens.

    Heights are scaled so that a sine of amplitude 1 centred on a bin reaches 1.0. The browser applies a Blackman window before the FFT, so a tone that falls between two bins reads up to about 12% lower. The speakers play the sum at one quarter of the displayed amplitude to protect listeners; the FFT analyses the full signal.

    Lesson Plan

    Learning Objectives

    By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:

    1. Identify the frequency and amplitude of each oscillator from the position and height of its peak in the magnitude spectrum
    2. Explain why the spectrum of a sum of two sinusoids contains one peak for each sinusoid (superposition)
    3. Predict how changing one oscillator's frequency or amplitude changes both the time-domain sum and the spectrum
    4. Demonstrate that the relative phase of the two components changes the shape of the sum but not its magnitude spectrum
    5. Relate the smallest frequency separation at which two peaks can be told apart to the FFT bin width

    Target Audience

    College undergraduates in an introductory signal processing course. The activities take about 25 minutes.

    Prerequisites

    • Sine and cosine functions; frequency, period and amplitude
    • The idea that an FFT shows which frequencies a signal contains (see the Basic FFT MicroSim)

    Activities

    The read-outs below were measured with a 48 kHz audio device. On a 44.1 kHz device the frequencies differ by up to about 3 Hz and the heights by a few hundredths, because the bins fall in different places.

    Activity 1: Two tones, two peaks (5 min). Keep the defaults (sine 440 Hz at 0.50, cosine 880 Hz at 0.30) and press Start. The spectrum shows two peaks; the read-out gives 439.5 Hz (0.49), 878.9 Hz (0.28). The sum repeats every \(1/440\) s = 2.27 ms, so the pattern appears 8.8 times in the 20 ms window. Max |x(t)| reads 0.80: the two waves reach \(-0.5\) and \(-0.3\) at the same instant, so the sum dips to \(-0.8\).

    Activity 2: Each slider controls its own peak (5 min).

    1. Set Cosine Amp to 0. Only the 439.5 Hz peak remains (0.49), and the time plot becomes a pure sine with a maximum of 0.50.
    2. Set Sine Amp to 0 and Cosine Amp back to 0.30. Only the 878.9 Hz peak remains (0.28); the maximum is 0.30.
    3. Set both amplitudes to 1.00. The peaks rise to 0.98 and 0.93, the time axis switches to \(\pm 2\), and Max |x(t)| reads 2.00 (both waves still reach \(-1\) at the same instant).

    Key point: changing one oscillator never moves or resizes the other oscillator's peak.

    Activity 3: Phase changes the shape, not the spectrum (5 min). Reload the page, press Start, and sketch the waveform (Max |x(t)| = 0.80). Drag Cosine Freq to 900 Hz and straight back to 880 Hz. The settings are the same as before, but the waveform usually has a different shape: in our tests Max |x(t)| read between 0.70 and 0.80 after a detour. The peaks still read 439.5 Hz (0.49) and 878.9 Hz (0.28). The detour shifted the relative phase of the two waves; for \(0.5\sin\theta + 0.3\sin(2\theta + \varphi)\) the maximum of \(|x|\) ranges from 0.69 to 0.80 depending on \(\varphi\), while the magnitude spectrum ignores \(\varphi\) completely.

    Activity 4: Beats and frequency resolution (7 min).

    1. Set the sine to 440 Hz at 0.50 and the cosine to 450 Hz at 0.50. You hear a tone that swells and fades 10 times a second (the beat frequency \(|f_2 - f_1| = 10\) Hz). In the time plot the wave's height pulses and Max |x(t)| jumps between about 0.3 and 1.0. The spectrum shows two separate peaks: 439.5 Hz (0.49) and 451.2 Hz (0.46).
    2. Set Cosine Freq to 446 Hz. There are still two peaks (439.5 Hz and 445.3 Hz), now only 6 Hz (about two bins) apart.
    3. Set Cosine Freq to 442 Hz. Now the spectrum shows a single peak at 442.4 Hz whose height rises and falls (roughly between 0.3 and 0.8) in step with the 2 beats per second you hear. The tones are closer together than the FFT can resolve.

    With 2.93 Hz bins and the Blackman window, two equal tones need to be about two bins (5-6 Hz) apart to appear as separate peaks. At 444-445 Hz the display flickers between one peak and two.

    Assessment

    1. The sine is set to 300 Hz at amplitude 0.50 and the cosine to 900 Hz at amplitude 0.25. Describe the spectrum. (Answer: two peaks, at 300 Hz and 900 Hz, the second about half as tall as the first; the MicroSim reads 298.8 Hz (0.46) and 899.4 Hz (0.25) at 48 kHz.)
    2. A cosine and a sine of the same frequency and amplitude produce the same peak. Why? (Answer: \(\cos\theta = \sin(\theta + 90^\circ)\), so the two differ only in phase, and the magnitude spectrum does not show phase.)
    3. With the defaults (440 Hz + 880 Hz), how often does the summed waveform repeat? (Answer: every \(1/440\) s = 2.27 ms. 880 Hz is the second harmonic of 440 Hz, so the sum has the period of the 440 Hz tone.)
    4. Two tones at 440 Hz and 447 Hz are played together. How many peaks does the FFT show, and how many beats per second do you hear? (Answer: two peaks, because 7 Hz is more than two bins (5.86 Hz) apart; the MicroSim reads 439.5 Hz and 448.2 Hz. You hear 7 beats per second.)
    5. Why does the 880 Hz peak read 0.28 instead of 0.30? (Answer: 880 Hz lies 0.37 of a bin away from the nearest bin centre (878.9 Hz). The Blackman window lowers the reading of a tone that falls between bins.)

    Common Misconceptions

    "Adding a second wave changes the first wave's spectrum." Each oscillator keeps its own peak. With the defaults running, move Cosine Amp from 0 to 1: the 878.9 Hz peak grows, while the 439.5 Hz peak stays at 0.49.

    "Two waveforms that look different must have different spectra." The shape of a sum depends on the relative phase of its components, but the magnitude spectrum does not. Activity 3 produces different-looking waveforms with identical peak read-outs.

    Development Notes

    This MicroSim was ported from p5.js 1.9 to p5.js 2.3.2 and p5.sound 0.4.1 in October 2026. Changes a returning user will notice:

    • Sound starts only after Start is pressed (browsers block audio until a click). The Pause button is now labelled Start while the tones are off.
    • The second oscillator is now a true cosine. The old library had no cosine type and silently played a sine.
    • The spectrum is plotted on a linear scale, so peak heights are proportional to the amplitudes. The old dB-style plot drew the default 0.50 and 0.30 tones at nearly the same height (235 and 217 out of 255).
    • The time plot gained a trigger, a 20 ms time axis, amplitude ticks and a Max |x(t)| read-out; the spectrum gained frequency ticks, peak markers and a peak read-out.
