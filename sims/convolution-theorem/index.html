    Convolution Theorem and Circular Convolution

    Run MicroSim in Fullscreen

    Description

    This MicroSim tests the convolution theorem for the discrete Fourier transform (DFT). The drop-down lists choose an input \(x[n]\) and an impulse response \(h[n]\): rectangle, triangle, exponential \(0.8^n\), alternating \((-1)^n\), a fixed random sequence, the impulse \(\delta[n]\), or a delayed impulse (a single 1 at the last sample). The N and M sliders set their lengths (1 to 16), and the Transform length L slider sets the DFT length, from \(\max(N, M)\) to 64. Both sequences are zero-padded to \(L\) samples.

    The panels, from top to bottom:

    • \(x[n]\) (blue) and \(h[n]\) (purple)
    • the linear convolution \(y[n] = \sum_k x[k]\,h[n-k]\) (green), with \(N + M - 1\) samples
    • the \(L\)-point circular convolution \(y_c[n]\) (teal), with gold rings for \(\mathrm{IDFT}_L\{X[k]H[k]\}\)
    • the error \(e[n] = y_c[n] - y[n]\) (red)
    • the magnitudes \(|X[k]|\), \(|H[k]|\) and \(|Y[k]|\); point at a bin to read its values

    Because the DFT treats each sequence as one period of a periodic signal, \(X[k]H[k]\) is the DFT of the circular convolution

    \[y_c[n] = \sum_{m} y[n + mL], \qquad 0 \le n < L .\]

    When \(L < N + M - 1\), the samples of \(y\) at \(n \ge L\) (red) do not fit in \(L\) samples: the arrows show them wrapping to \(n - L\) and adding to the samples there (time aliasing). The read-outs give the required length, the maximum error, and whether circular equals linear, which holds whenever \(L \ge N + M - 1\). The spectra are scaled by \(\max|X|\), \(\max|H|\) and \(\max|X|\cdot\max|H|\), so each maroon stem is the blue height times the purple height.

    This MicroSim is used in Chapter 4: Convolution and Correlation.

    Lesson Plan

    Learning Objectives

    After using this MicroSim, students will be able to:

    1. Calculate the length \(N + M - 1\) of a linear convolution and the smallest DFT length \(L\) for which \(\mathrm{IDFT}_L\{X[k]H[k]\}\) equals it
    2. Explain circular convolution as linear convolution with its tail wrapped around, \(y_c[n] = \sum_m y[n + mL]\), and identify the time-aliased samples when \(L < N + M - 1\)
    3. Verify the convolution theorem \(Y[k] = X[k]H[k]\) by comparing the DFT route with the wrap-around sum and by reading \(|X[k]|\), \(|H[k]|\) and \(|Y[k]|\) at one bin
    4. Evaluate whether a transform length \(L\) is adequate for a given pair of sequences, including cases where the wrapped samples happen to be zero
    5. Predict that a delay becomes a circular shift (rotation) when \(L\) is too short

    Target Audience

    Undergraduate students in a signals and systems or digital signal processing course (typically junior year). Plan 25 minutes for the activities below.

    Prerequisites

    • The convolution sum \(y[n] = \sum_k x[k]\,h[n-k]\)
    • The definition of the DFT and its inverse
    • Magnitude of a complex number

    Activities

    Activity 1: Watch the tail wrap around (6 min). Start with the defaults: triangle \(x\) with \(N = 8\) (samples 1, 2, 3, 4, 4, 3, 2, 1), rectangle \(h\) with \(M = 4\), and \(L = 8\). The read-out says that \(L \ge 8 + 4 - 1 = 11\) is required. The linear result is 1, 3, 6, 10, 13, 14, 13, 10, 6, 3, 1. Its last three samples (6, 3 and 1, in red) wrap to \(n\) = 0, 1 and 2, so \(y_c\) = 7, 6, 7, 10, 13, 14, 13, 10 and the labels read 1+6=7, 3+3=6 and 6+1=7. The error panel shows +6, +3, +1 at the start and −6, −3, −1 at \(n\) = 8 to 10, so the maximum error is 6. Now raise \(L\) one step at a time: at \(L = 9\) the maximum error is 3, at \(L = 10\) it is 1, and at \(L = 11\) the status line turns green with a maximum error of 0. At \(L = 16\) the error is still 0; the extra samples are zeros.

    Activity 2: The theorem in the frequency domain (6 min). Return to \(L = 8\). The gold rings sit on every circular sample, the red ones included, even though the result is wrong: in a window at least 700 px wide the circular-panel legend reports that the two routes differ by 1.8e−15, which is rounding error. Point at bin \(k = 0\): the read-out shows \(|X[0]| = 20.00\), \(|H[0]| = 4.00\) and \(|Y[0]| = 80.00\), the sums of \(x\), \(h\) and \(y_c\). Now choose the exponential for \(x\) and point at \(k = 2\): \(|X[2]| = 0.65\) but \(|H[2]| = 0.00\), so \(|Y[2]| = 0.00\). A 4-sample rectangle padded to 8 samples has zeros at \(k\) = 2, 4 and 6, and the product inherits every zero.

    Activity 3: A delay becomes a rotation (5 min). Choose the triangle for \(x\) (\(N = 8\)) and the delayed impulse for \(h\) (\(M = 4\), so \(h[n] = \delta[n-3]\)), with \(L = 8\). The linear result is \(x\) delayed by three samples (0, 0, 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 4, 3, 2, 1), but the circular result is 3, 2, 1, 1, 2, 3, 4, 4: the last three samples have come around to the front, so the maximum error is 3. In the spectrum every purple stem has \(|H[k]| = 1\), because a delay changes only the phase, and the maroon stems match the blue ones. Set \(L = 11\) and the circular result becomes the true delay.

    Activity 4: What the length rule really requires (5 min). Choose the impulse \(\delta[n]\) for \(x\) (\(N = 8\)) and the rectangle for \(h\) (\(M = 4\)) with \(L = 8\). The required length is still 11, but the status line is green: the three samples that wrap are all 0. Next choose the rectangle for both sequences with \(N = M = 16\): the required length is 31. At \(L = 16\) every circular sample equals 16 and the maximum error is 15. At \(L = 30\) only \(y[30] = 1\) wraps, so \(y_c[0] = 1 + 1 = 2\) and the maximum error is 1. At \(L = 31\), or at the FFT-friendly \(L = 32\), the error is 0.

    Assessment

    1. \(x[n]\) has 12 samples and \(h[n]\) has 5. What is the smallest \(L\) for which \(\mathrm{IDFT}_L\{X[k]H[k]\}\) equals the linear convolution, and what goes wrong at one less? Answer: \(L = 12 + 5 - 1 = 16\). At \(L = 15\) the last sample \(y[15]\) wraps onto \(y_c[0]\) (with the triangle and rectangle the sim shows a maximum error of 1).
    2. With the default settings (triangle \(N = 8\), rectangle \(M = 4\), \(L = 8\)), compute \(y_c[1]\) by hand. Answer: \(y_c[1] = y[1] + y[9] = 3 + 3 = 6\).
    3. If \(L = N + M - 2\), how many samples of the circular result are wrong? Answer: one. Only \(y[N + M - 2]\) wraps, so \(y_c[0]\) is too large by that amount and the last linear sample is missing (with the defaults and \(L = 10\): \(y_c[0] = 1 + 1 = 2\)).
    4. The gold rings always land on the circular result, even when the error panel is red. What does this show? Answer: the product of two \(L\)-point DFTs is always exactly the DFT of the circular convolution, for every \(L\). The theorem never fails; what fails for short \(L\) is the assumption that circular convolution equals linear convolution.
    5. With the 4-sample rectangle and \(L = 8\), \(|H[2]| = 0\). What is \(|Y[2]|\) for any input \(x\), and why? Answer: 0, because \(Y[k] = X[k]H[k]\) and a zero of either factor is a zero of the product.

    Common Misconceptions

    "Multiplying two FFTs gives the convolution." It gives the circular convolution. With the defaults and \(L = 8\) the gold rings agree with the wrap-around sum to about \(10^{-15}\), yet the error panel shows a maximum error of 6 against the linear result. The two agree only when \(L \ge N + M - 1\).

    "Extra zero padding changes the answer." Compare \(L = 11\), \(L = 16\) and \(L = 64\) with the defaults: the same 11 samples appear, followed by zeros, and the maximum error stays 0. More padding only samples the spectrum at more frequencies; it does not change \(y[n]\).
