    Description

    This MicroSim generates a pure sine tone \(x(t) = A\sin(2\pi f t)\), plays it through your speakers, and shows its magnitude spectrum from 0 to 10 kHz, computed live by a Fast Fourier Transform (FFT). Each blue bar shows the strongest FFT bin in a narrow band of frequencies. Heights are levels in decibels, \(L = 20\log_{10}(A)\) dB, so the top of the plot (0 dB) is a sine of amplitude 1.

    • Start/Stop turns the tone on and off. Nothing plays until you press Start.
    • Frequency (20 Hz to 10 kHz, default 440 Hz) sets \(f\). You can also press and drag horizontally across the plot: the frequency under the pointer is applied, so the peak follows the pointer.
    • Amplitude (0 to 0.50, default 0.20) sets \(A\). The default is deliberately quiet; set your volume low before you press Start.

    The dashed line marks the frequency you set. The gold triangle marks the strongest FFT bin, and the read-out above the plot gives that bin's frequency and level. A tone of amplitude 0.20 reads -14.0 dB. The FFT uses \(N = 8192\) points, so it measures only at bins \(\Delta f = f_s/N\) apart: 5.86 Hz at a 48 kHz sample rate (5.38 Hz at 44.1 kHz). The read-out therefore differs from the set frequency by up to half a bin, and a tone that falls between two bins reads up to about 1 dB low because the browser applies a Blackman window. The sample rate and bin width are printed under the plot on wide screens.

    This MicroSim supports Chapter 2: Signal Frequency and previews the spectra of Chapter 7: DFT, FFT and Frequency Domain Analysis.

    Lesson Plan

    Learning Objectives

    By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:

    1. Identify the single spectral peak that a pure sine tone produces in the FFT display and read its frequency and level
    2. Predict how the FFT peak moves along the frequency axis as the tone sweeps from low to high frequency, and relate its position to the pitch they hear
    3. Explain how horizontal position on the plot maps linearly to a frequency between 20 Hz and 10 kHz
    4. Calculate the peak level \(20\log_{10}(A)\) dB for an amplitude \(A\) and verify that halving \(A\) lowers the peak by about 6 dB
    5. Explain why the read-out can differ from the set frequency by up to half a bin width (2.93 Hz at 48 kHz)

    Target Audience

    College undergraduates in an introductory signal processing course. The activities take about 20 minutes.

    Prerequisites

    • Sine waves: amplitude, frequency and period
    • Sound as a pressure wave; frequency and pitch
    • Base-10 logarithms (for decibels)

    Activities

    The read-outs below were measured with a 48 kHz audio device. On a 44.1 kHz device the bins are 5.38 Hz apart, so the frequency read-outs differ from these by a few hertz; each is still within half a bin of the set frequency.

    Activity 1: Hear the pitch, see the peak (5 min).

    1. Set your volume low and press Start (440 Hz, amplitude 0.20). One narrow peak appears on the dashed line, and the read-out shows FFT peak: 439.5 Hz, -14.0 dB.
    2. Set the frequency to 220 Hz. The pitch drops by one octave and the peak moves left (read-out 222.7 Hz).
    3. Set 880 Hz (read-out 878.9 Hz), then 1760 Hz (read-out 1757.8 Hz). Each doubling raises the pitch by one octave and doubles the peak's distance from 0 Hz.

    Activity 2: Drag the tone across the spectrum (5 min). Press on the plot at the 1 kHz tick and drag slowly to the 6 kHz tick. The frequency label follows the pointer and the peak stays under it; release on the 6 kHz tick and the read-out shows close to 6000 Hz. Then compare two equal 1 kHz steps: drag from the 1 kHz tick to the 2 kHz tick, then from the 8 kHz tick to the 9 kHz tick. Both steps are the same width on the linear axis, but the first is a full octave (ratio 2) while the second is a ratio of only 1.125, about two semitones, so the first sounds like a much larger change in pitch.

    Activity 3: Amplitude and decibels (5 min). Set the frequency to 1500 Hz (exactly on a bin at 48 kHz) and complete the table, then compare with \(20\log_{10}(A)\).

    Amplitude Predicted level (dB) Read-out level (dB)
    0.50
    0.40
    0.20
    0.10
    0.05

    Expected read-outs: -6.0, -8.0, -14.0, -20.0 and -26.0 dB. Each halving of the amplitude (0.40 to 0.20, 0.20 to 0.10, 0.10 to 0.05) lowers the peak by 6.0 dB, and the peak never moves sideways.

    Activity 4: Frequency resolution (5 min). Read the bin width under the plot (\(48000 / 8192 = 5.86\) Hz). Set 1500 Hz: the read-out is exactly 1500.0 Hz because \(1500 / 5.859375 = 256\) is a whole bin. Set 1000 Hz: the read-out is 1002.0 Hz, the centre of bin 171, because \(1000 / 5.859375 = 170.7\). Set 220 Hz: the read-out is 222.7 Hz (bin 38). In every case the error is at most half a bin, 2.93 Hz.

    Assessment

    1. The amplitude is set to 0.25. What level will the FFT peak show at 1500 Hz? (Answer: \(20\log_{10}(0.25) = -12.0\) dB; the read-out shows -12.0 dB.)
    2. With \(f_s = 48\) kHz and \(N = 8192\), what is the bin width, and why does a 1000 Hz tone read 1002.0 Hz? (Answer: \(48000/8192 = 5.86\) Hz. 1000 Hz lies between bin 170 (996.1 Hz) and bin 171 (1002.0 Hz); it is closer to bin 171, so that bin is the strongest.)
    3. You change the frequency from 440 Hz to 880 Hz without touching the amplitude. What happens to the pitch, the position of the peak and its height? (Answer: the pitch rises one octave, the peak moves right to twice the frequency (878.9 Hz), and the height stays the same (-14.0 dB before, -14.1 dB after).)
    4. The plot spans 0 to 10 kHz across its width. Where must you press to set about 2500 Hz? (Answer: one quarter of the way from the 0 tick to the 10 kHz tick, halfway between the 2 and 3 kHz ticks, because the axis is linear.)
    5. After you press Stop, the peak sinks and disappears within a fraction of a second. What does this tell you about where the peak comes from? (Answer: the FFT only reveals frequencies that are present in the signal; when the tone is silent there is nothing at 440 Hz to show.)

    Common Misconceptions

    "A louder tone has a higher frequency." Amplitude and frequency are independent. At 440 Hz, change the amplitude from 0.10 to 0.40: the peak rises from -20.0 dB to -8.0 dB but stays at 439.5 Hz, and the pitch does not change.

    "Doubling the amplitude doubles the level in decibels." Decibels are logarithmic: doubling the amplitude adds \(20\log_{10}(2) = 6.0\) dB. Activity 3 shows -20.0 dB at 0.10 and -14.0 dB at 0.20, not -40 dB.

    "The FFT measures the exact frequency." It measures the energy in bins a fixed distance apart. A 1000 Hz tone reads 1002.0 Hz at 48 kHz because the nearest bin centre is at 1002.0 Hz.
