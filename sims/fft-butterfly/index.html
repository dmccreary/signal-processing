    Description

    This MicroSim traces the first job of a radix-2 decimation-in-time FFT: putting the \(N\) input samples into bit-reversed order before the butterfly stages combine them. Each circle is one sample \(x[n]\) labelled with its index \(n\) (at \(N = 32\), read the binary labels). The orange column is the natural order \(x[0], x[1], \dots, x[N-1]\).

    • Start / Next Step adds one column. Each step splits every group into its even-indexed samples (top half of the group) and its odd-indexed samples (bottom half). Navy lines show where each sample moves, and gray bars mark the boundaries of the new groups.
    • Reset returns to the natural order.
    • FFT Size (4, 8, 16 or 32) chooses \(N\) and resets the diagram.

    The diagram has \(\log_2 N\) columns: the natural order plus \(\log_2 N - 1\) splits. The last split would only turn pairs into single samples, which does not change the order. The green column therefore holds \(N/2\) pairs of samples that are \(N/2\) apart, such as \(x[0], x[4]\) for \(N = 8\); each pair feeds one butterfly of the first stage. The binary labels show why the result is called bit-reversed: read across a row and the bits on the right are the bits on the left in reverse order. For \(N = 8\), row 1 (001) receives \(x[4]\) (100).

    Each split rests on the decimation-in-time identity, where \(E[k]\) and \(O[k]\) are the \(N/2\)-point DFTs of the even and odd samples:

    \[X[k] = E[k] + W_N^k\,O[k], \qquad X[k + N/2] = E[k] - W_N^k\,O[k], \qquad W_N = e^{-j2\pi/N}\]

    Repeating the split \(\log_2 N\) times costs \((N/2)\log_2 N\) complex multiplications instead of \(N^2\): 12 instead of 64 for \(N = 8\), and 80 instead of 1024 for \(N = 32\).

    This MicroSim supports Chapter 6: Fourier Analysis Fundamentals.

    Lesson Plan

    Learning Objectives

    After using this MicroSim, students will be able to:

    1. Describe one decimation-in-time step as splitting every group of samples into its even-indexed and odd-indexed halves
    2. Calculate the number of columns (\(\log_2 N\)), the number of splits (\(\log_2 N - 1\)) and the group size after split \(s\) (\(N/2^s\)) for a given FFT size
    3. Predict the row that sample \(x[n]\) occupies in the final column by reversing the bits of \(n\), and verify the prediction in the diagram
    4. Explain why the final pairs \(x[m]\) and \(x[m + N/2]\) are the inputs of the first butterfly stage
    5. Compare the \((N/2)\log_2 N\) complex multiplications of the radix-2 FFT with the \(N^2\) of the direct DFT

    Target Audience

    College undergraduates in an introductory digital signal processing course, during the lesson on the FFT. Plan 15-20 minutes.

    Prerequisites

    • The definition of the DFT and its \(N^2\) cost
    • Binary representation of integers
    • Even and odd indices of a sequence

    Activities

    Activity 1: One split at a time (5 min). Keep FFT Size at 8 and press Start. The middle column holds 0, 2, 4, 6 above the gray bar and 1, 3, 5, 7 below it: the even and the odd samples. Press Next Step. Each group of 4 splits again and the green column reads 0, 4, 2, 6, 1, 5, 3, 7. Ask: which samples feed each 4-point DFT, and which feed each 2-point DFT?

    Activity 2: Read the bits (5 min). At the last step for \(N = 8\), compare the binary labels across each row. Row 3 shows 011 on the left and 110 on the right, so it receives \(x[6]\). Find the samples that end in the row where they started: \(x[0]\), \(x[2]\), \(x[5]\) and \(x[7]\), whose 3-bit patterns (000, 010, 101, 111) read the same in both directions.

    Activity 3: Count the steps (5 min). Select each FFT Size and press Next Step until nothing changes. The title stops at Step 2 of 2 for \(N = 4\), Step 3 of 3 for \(N = 8\), Step 4 of 4 for \(N = 16\) and Step 5 of 5 for \(N = 32\). Record \(N\), the number of splits and the group size after each split. Each doubling of \(N\) adds one split, and one butterfly stage.

    Activity 4: Predict before you click (5 min). Select \(N = 16\). Before stepping, reverse four bits to predict the final rows: \(x[3]\) (0011) goes to row 12 (1100), \(x[5]\) (0101) to row 10 (1010) and \(x[11]\) (1011) to row 13 (1101). Step to the end and check the green column. Which four samples end in their own row? (\(x[0]\), \(x[6]\), \(x[9]\) and \(x[15]\).)

    Assessment

    1. For \(N = 32\), how many splits does the diagram show, and what are the groups after the second split? (Answer: \(\log_2 32 - 1 = 4\) splits; after the second split there are 4 groups of 8 samples.)
    2. For \(N = 16\), which sample sits in row 2 of the final column? (Answer: row 2 is 0010; reversed it is 0100 = 4, so \(x[4]\). The green column reads 0, 8, 4, 12, ...)
    3. After the first split for \(N = 8\), the top group is \(x[0], x[2], x[4], x[6]\). What is computed from these samples, and how is it combined with the bottom group? (Answer: the 4-point DFT \(E[k]\) of the even samples. It is combined with the odd-sample DFT \(O[k]\) as \(X[k] = E[k] + W_8^k O[k]\) and \(X[k+4] = E[k] - W_8^k O[k]\).)
    4. How many complex multiplications does a radix-2 FFT need for \(N = 32\), compared with the direct DFT? (Answer: \((32/2) \times 5 = 80\) versus \(32^2 = 1024\), about 13 times fewer.)
    5. Is the green column the FFT output \(X[k]\)? (Answer: no. It holds the same input samples in a new order; no arithmetic has been done yet. The butterfly stages that follow compute \(X[k]\), which comes out in natural order.)

    Common Misconceptions

    "Bit-reversed order means the list is reversed." Reversing the list would put \(x[7]\) first. In the diagram \(x[0]\) and \(x[7]\) stay at the top and bottom while \(x[4]\) moves up to row 1: the bits of each index are reversed, not the list.

    "The right-hand column is the FFT result." The green circles carry the same sample labels as the orange ones. Reordering only moves data; the multiplications and additions happen in the butterfly stages that follow.

    References

    1. Fast Fourier transform - Wikipedia - overview of FFT algorithms and their \(O(N \log N)\) cost
    2. Cooley-Tukey FFT algorithm - Wikipedia - the radix-2 decimation-in-time recursion into even and odd samples
    3. Bit-reversal permutation - Wikipedia - the input order shown in the green column and how it is computed
    4. Butterfly diagram - Wikipedia - the two-input butterfly that combines each pair after the reordering
    5. The Scientist and Engineer's Guide to Digital Signal Processing - Steven W. Smith - free online textbook; Chapter 12 explains the FFT's interlaced decomposition and bit-reversal sorting

    Development Notes

    Version 2.0 (October 2026) ported the sketch to p5.js 2.3.2 and removed the unused p5.sound library. The intermediate columns now follow the textbook decimation-in-time split (whole sequence first, then each half); the earlier version built the same bit-reversed order from the smallest groups upward. The binary labels, the group bars and the caption under the diagram were added, and the column headings were renamed from "Inputs"/"Outputs" to "Natural order"/"Bit-reversed order", because the right-hand column is not the FFT output.
